AppBlips can put your project online for you. It uses [Surge](https://surge.sh), a free hosting service. Your project gets its own web address, such as `https://my-bakery-x7k2.surge.sh`, that anyone can open. The address uses HTTPS, the padlock in the address bar that keeps the connection secure.

Publishing works for apps, websites and games. Imported sites can't be published yet. Download them with [Export](/features/importing-sites#export-the-project) instead.

## Publish for the first time

You need a free Surge account. AppBlips creates one for you the first time you sign in.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Open the Publish window">
    In the preview toolbar, click **Publish** (the globe button near the right end).
  </Step>
  <Step title="Sign in to Surge">
    - **New to Surge:** click **Create an account**. Type your email, choose a password, type it again in **Type the password again**, then click **Create account**.
    - **Already have a Surge account:** leave **I have an account** selected, type your email and password, then click **Sign in**.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Choose the web address">
    Under **Web address**, AppBlips suggests a name based on your project. Keep it or type your own. Use only lowercase letters, numbers and dashes.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Decide whether search engines can find it">
    Tick **Let search engines find this site** if you want Google and others to list it. Leave it unticked (the default) and AppBlips publishes a `robots.txt` and a "noindex" tag asking search engines to stay away. Anyone with the link can still open the site, and well-behaved search engines respect the request, but it isn't a password. AppBlips remembers your choice for each project, and you can change it later with **Update site**.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Add a password (optional)">
    Tick **Password protect this site**, type a password (at least 10 characters), then type it again. Visitors then see a lock screen and must type the password before they can see anything. See [Password protection](#password-protection).
  </Step>
  <Step title="Choose a site icon (optional)">
    Under **Site icon**, click **Choose image…** and pick a picture from your computer (PNG, JPEG, WebP, GIF, SVG or ICO). It's the small picture people see in the browser tab, in bookmarks and on a phone's home screen. A square image works best. To go back to no icon, click **Remove**.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Publish">
    Click **Publish**. After a few seconds, the window shows **Your site is live** and the link. Click **Copy** to copy the link, or **Open site** to visit it.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Share it (optional)">
    Under **Share it**, click one of the icons: email, X, Facebook, LinkedIn, Reddit, Bluesky or WhatsApp (point at an icon to see its name). Your browser opens that service's share page (or your email app) with the link filled in. Nothing is posted until you post it there.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Submit it to the Showcase (optional)">
    Click **Submit it to the showcase** to send your project to the AppBlips team, with its link already filled in. Approved projects are featured in the Showcase. See [Showcase](/features/showcase#submit-your-project).
  </Step>
</Steps>

<Note>
  Unless you add a [password](#password-protection), anyone with the link can see your published site. Don't publish anything private.
</Note>

## Password protection

When you tick **Password protect this site**, AppBlips encrypts every page on your computer before it uploads anything. What goes online is a lock screen with the scrambled pages inside it.

- **Your password is never uploaded or saved.** Not by AppBlips and not in the published files. Visitors' browsers use what they type to unlock the pages.
- **Your project's code stays hidden until the password is entered.** Pictures are locked too, and the search-engine sitemap isn't published. Only the site icon stays public, because the lock screen shows it.
- **Visitors unlock once.** Their browser remembers the unlock for that tab, so the site's other pages open without asking again. Closing the tab locks it again.
- **Update site asks for the password again.** AppBlips doesn't keep it. Type the same one to keep it, or a new one to change it. Untick the box and update to remove the password.

<Warning>
  The site is on a static host, which can't count wrong guesses or lock anyone out. Someone who downloads the locked pages could try many passwords on their own computer. AppBlips makes each guess slow, but a short or common password can still be guessed. Use a long password, such as four random words, and don't reuse one from elsewhere. Once someone has the password and has unlocked the site, they can see everything on it.
</Warning>

## Update a published site

After you change your project, click **Publish** in the preview toolbar again. For a project that's already online, the window shows **This project is online** and its address.

- **Update site:** replaces what's online with your latest version, at the same address. If the site has a password, type it again first.
- **Site icon:** change or remove the icon, then click **Update site** to put the change online. AppBlips remembers the icon for each project.
- **Unpublish:** takes the site offline. See [Remove a published site](#remove-a-published-site).
- **Publish to a different address:** puts a second copy online at a new address. The old address stays online until you unpublish it.

## If the address is taken

Surge addresses are first come, first served. If someone else already uses the name, the window says it belongs to another Surge account. Type a different name and click **Publish** again.

## Your Surge sign-in

- Your password goes to Surge once and is never saved. AppBlips keeps only the sign-in that Surge sends back.
  - In the desktop app, that sign-in is encrypted with your computer's keychain.
  - In the browser version, it's saved on your computer in a file only your user account can read. It's never saved in the browser.
- To sign in ahead of time, or to sign out, open **Settings → Connectors** and click the **Surge** card. You can also click **Sign out** in the Publish window.
- Surge sends a confirmation email when you create an account. You can publish without confirming it, but confirmed accounts can publish more often.
- If you forget your password, reset it at [surge.sh](https://surge.sh).

## Remove a published site

1. Open the project and click **Publish** in the preview toolbar.
2. Click **Unpublish**.
3. Click **Unpublish** again to confirm.

The site goes offline and the link stops working. Your project stays in AppBlips, so you can click **Publish again** later. Someone else could take the address in the meantime.

AppBlips only remembers the last address you published each project to. To remove an older address, install [Node.js](https://nodejs.org), open a terminal, type `npx surge teardown old-name.surge.sh` (using your own address) and press Enter, then sign in with your Surge email and password.

## Limits

- Password protection needs a secure (`https`) address, which every `surge.sh` address is. A protected site isn't listed by search engines, whatever **Let search engines find this site** says.
- Surge's free plan includes HTTPS on every `surge.sh` address. Custom domains, such as `www.mybakery.com`, aren't supported in AppBlips yet.
- Surge limits how often you can publish in a short time. If you see a "rate limited" message, wait 15 minutes and try again.
- Surge's [terms](https://surge.sh/terms) apply to what you publish.
- Images made with [Pollinations](/features/connectors#pollinations) are copied into your site when you publish, so they stay online as long as it does. Publish again after the AI changes an image.
- If the [SEO connector](/features/connectors#seo) is on and has your website address, a `sitemap.xml` and `robots.txt` are published with the site, but only when **Let search engines find this site** is ticked. The box overrides the connector's own indexing switch when you publish.
- Your own images from [My images](/features/building-apps#using-your-own-images-beta) are uploaded with the site, but only the ones your pages use. A site's images can be at most 60 MB in total.
